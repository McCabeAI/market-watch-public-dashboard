## Scope and source selection

The reference contains **58 retrievable sections and 46 supporting source documents**. A source document is counted once even when several chapters or pages support it. Publisher announcements, access-failure candidates, operating rules and legal/terms pages are excluded from that count. The source inventory below is derived from the canonical document’s source register; that register controls IDs, URLs and section locators.

The research began with the six requested source families. Current exchange rules control contract specifications; current central-bank methodologies control benchmark definitions. Older educational works support enduring concepts only where accessible and corroborated. Additional sources fill specific gaps: CFTC/NFA for independent margin and liquidation mechanics; Treasury/FINRA for bond pricing and duration; Bank of England for OIS and expectation measurement; OCC for options; current CME rulebooks and focused lessons for contract arithmetic. No broker marketing, trading blog, social-media post, unauthorized mirror or trader lesson supplies evidence. The New York Fed’s institutional Liberty Street Economics research is distinguished from a trading blog.

The Global Operating Rules, Market Watch Operating Hub and AI Writing Avoid List were read before substantive authoring and reused. No other specialist trading standard was loaded. Project-specific style, instrument preference and research hierarchy were not imported as market doctrine. No trader/PM cycle, infrastructure change, agent integration or merge is part of this work.

## Material ambiguities and resolutions

| Issue challenged | Resolution in the canonical reference |
|---|---|
| Older FX dealer-market descriptions versus current structure | Use 2025 BIS survey research and the 2024 New York Fed account for electronic venues, internalisation and non-bank participants. Avoid timeless market-share claims and avoid copying Cross without reading it. |
| CIP equality versus observed basis | State replication assumptions and quote orientation. S07 explains departures relative to benchmark funding curves. No assumption of universally executable frictionless arbitrage. |
| Forward points and inverse quotes | Points equal forward minus spot only in the declared orientation and units. Reciprocal quotes are inverted mathematically, not by changing a sign. S09’s simplified additive carry wording is not used as an exact formula; its dated quote table is not copied. |
| OIS as expectation measure versus risk premia | S13 identifies possible premia and estimates their significance in a historical sample. The reference preserves the caveat without claiming that OIS is unusable or that premia are always zero. |
| All short-rate instruments treated as one benchmark | S14 documents the mixed benchmark landscape. SOFR, EFFR, a policy target and a term benchmark are not equated. |
| SR3 named month versus rulebook delivery month | Name starts the reference quarter; rulebook delivery month occurs at its end. Explicitly stated and checked against Chapter 460 and S15. |
| SOFR equivalent notional and remaining fixing risk | Use contractual BPV and actual days. A USD 1 million label is a 90-day equivalent, not a loan delivered by the future. Already fixed days cannot respond to later shocks. |
| Futures linearity versus convexity adjustment | A fixed USD-per-bp quoted-rate payout is linear in that quote. It does not make futures and discounted swap cash flows identical, or imply zero curvature with respect to every curve input. S45 is used only for that narrow distinction; its “short” swap example is not used to define receive/pay signs. |
| Old versus current Treasury deliverable basket | Current CBOT Chapter 19 takes precedence: 6½ years to less than 8 years remaining, subject to its rounding/eligibility terms, verified 2026-10-02. Older 6½-to-10-year educational descriptions are not current specifications. |
| CTD, net basis and implied repo | Compare consistent financing and delivery assumptions. Lowest gross basis alone does not universally identify the economic CTD. No current CTD is named. |
| CF multiplied versus divided in futures DV01 | Divide contract-face CTD DV01 by CF under the stated approximation. Numeric hedge example checked independently from the prose. |
| Yield, coupon and realized compound return | Distinguish current yield, YTM as IRR and terminal wealth after reinvestment. Computing YTM does not require predicting reinvestment rates. Treasury’s short “annual return” description is not promoted to a guaranteed realized-return claim. |
| Signed DV01/PV01 and fly conventions | Define positive DV01 magnitude and separately describe signed +1 bp value changes. Declare butterfly weights and quote sign; no universal “long fly” convention assumed. |
| Carry, roll and P&L overlap | Use a complete remaining-value-plus-cash reconciliation. State the aging/curve scenario, exclude capital contributions, and prohibit double counting of coupons, accrued interest or settled futures variation. |
| Exchange education includes advice or simplifications | Exclude promotion, strategy selection, old hedge ratios and causal macro narratives. S31 has inconsistent rounded/example labels; own dimensionally consistent examples replace them. Current rules control over education. |
| Options shorthand overstates generality | Greek signs qualified for standard vanilla/model conditions. Delta is not asserted to be a universal exercise probability. Nonnegative extrinsic value and negative theta are not imposed across all exercise styles/rate configurations. |
| Regulatory examples outside their scope | NFA security-futures percentages and deadlines do not become margin specifications for SOFR or Treasury futures. CFTC 2017 stop-protection bands do not become current venue rules. |

## Required four-case mechanical tests

These are tests of the reference, not imported trader memories. The situations below come from the task’s QA requirements; no historical decision record was used as evidence.

| Test | Relevant canonical IDs | Expected baseline diagnosis | Result and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receiving/buying a hike-priced strip above cash is called a defensive slowdown expression | `shared.direction`, `rates.futures.stir`, `rates.strips.cash-path`, `rates.sofr.fixings` | A long 100-minus-rate position benefits from lower benchmark rates than priced. Above-cash implied rates can therefore be faded even if realized rates rise from current cash. Known fixings, window and benchmark must be identified. | **Pass.** “Slowdown” and “fade of priced tightening” can coexist; the error is failure to recognize exposure, not the narrative label alone. No universal rule says the trade is attractive or unattractive. |
| Unchanged quoted levels are treated as zero economic P&L | `pnl.decomposition.cashflows`, `pnl.flat-marks`, `bonds.returns.carry-roll` | Clean quotes omit accrual; cash flows, funding and time can matter. Futures variation must not be counted twice. | **Pass.** Includes a +400 hypothetical funded-bond reconciliation and the limiting case: unchanged complete values with zero net flows/costs really do imply zero P&L. |
| Long-horizon valuation is treated as identical to a short event-window trade | `shared.time`, `trade.time.horizon`, `rates.sofr.sr3` | Holding-period exit price, fixing window, maturity and expiry determine different exposures. A later convergence premise does not supply the earlier exit price. | **Pass.** Timing distinction is mechanical. Whether an event will cause convergence remains contextual research. |
| Prior thesis invalidation is treated as sufficient reason for automatic reversal | `trade.positions.close-reverse`, `meta.baseline-experience` | Closing +q requires −q; reversing to −q requires a −2q transaction. Opposite exposure and new costs are separate from eliminating exposure. | **Pass with explicit boundary.** Mechanics identifies the transaction/exposure difference. It cannot alone judge whether the new opposite thesis is supported. No “never reverse” or “always reverse” doctrine is introduced. |

## Bias and independent challenge pass

A separate adversarial self-review followed drafting; this was not an external expert review. It checked the finished text against the task’s challenge list rather than treating a source’s authority as sufficient proof.

| Challenge | Outcome |
|---|---|
| House/trader bias or current positions | No house preference, current position, personality, model-routing instruction or experiential lesson used as evidence. Directional examples are hypothetical and symmetric where sign matters. |
| Source monoculture | 23 of 46 sources are CME/CBOT, reflecting the required exchange products. Nine sections cite only that family, principally exact contract/quote mechanics. Other sources include central banks, BIS, GFXC, Treasury, CFTC, FINRA, NFA, World Bank/IMF and OCC. Exchange material does not set room-wide strategy. |

## Validation and handoff

Repository workflow triggers were inspected before handoff. These documentation paths do not invoke path-gated trading/PM workflows. Broad pull-request workflows contain validation/unit-test jobs; live launch and ingestion jobs are guarded for manual/issue events. No live dispatch is performed.
