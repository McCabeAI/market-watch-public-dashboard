# Labor, inflation, and the four conditional stories

US payrolls, unemployment, and wages are the current release for observation period 2026-09. The US labor temperature score is level 51.26, impulse -1.54, direction cooling, class neutral, as of 2026-09, coverage 1. Its lineage sets 50 as the anchor. The label on the score is that it is not a raw print, and the score is marked not a consensus. A level of 51.26 is a labor state near that anchor, with a mild cooling impulse. It is not a growth collapse, and it is not the employment report the 5 November and 6 November headlines are waiting on. Component rows for the Chicago Fed labor fields sit around 2026-10, including a nowcast final marked new and a nowcast revision marked new. Initial claims are a new observation for 2026-10-31, and continuing claims for 2026-10-24. Those rows carry status and period. They do not carry numeric values here, so no claims level and no nowcast level is stated.

US core PCE is source_failed, previous release retained, observation period 2026-07. The inflation temperature score matches that staleness: level 63.1, impulse -0.81, direction static, class warm, as of 2026-07, not a raw print, not a consensus. Warm and static, on a July retention, does not tell me what inflation did into November. CPI core is a current row for 2026-09 and the row has no number. The activity score for reference period 2026-10 is level 50.77, impulse -3.59, direction cooling, class neutral. That is a cooling impulse near the anchor, and it is a score.

Canadian labor components for employment, unemployment, and wages are source_failed and unavailable. A Canadian labor temperature block is present, as of 2026-09, with component release status inherited or inconsistent. It is not used as a Canadian print.

These four stories are conditional accounts of how the curve would be wrong. They are not forecasts of a missing release.

If US employment were strong, the curve that would be wrong is the front-end rally read as growth. SR3H7 at 4.24 would be too low, and the declined receive would be the losing expression. The review level for that story is 4.53.

If US employment were weak, the curve that would be wrong is the 30 bp gap between the March period and the overnight fixing, read as a path that can be faded. The declined pay would be the losing expression if the implied rate fell toward 3.94. The gap would still not have been carry.

If Canadian employment were strong, the curve story that would be wrong is a continental growth shock used to receive US March SOFR. The US period would still have to be judged on US evidence. This does not become a Canada rates decision.

If Canadian employment were weak, the curve story that would be wrong is a purely domestic fade of the US rally. The pay would have been early if the shock were broader than the United States. The Canadian components that would show that are unavailable, so the conditional does not fill the US hole and does not identify SR3H7.

Incomplete evidence narrows the conclusion. A near-anchor September labor score, a stale warm inflation score, claims rows without numbers, and no October or November employment-situation print leave both the strong and the weak US stories open. That is a reason to wait, not a reason to average them into a smaller receive or a smaller pay.
